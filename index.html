Whole-arm trajectory augmentation

Object positions are sampled 0.50–0.88 m from the calibrated robot base projected onto the tabletop plane, within a 65–130° sector intersected with the usable tabletop. Angles are measured from tabletop +X (along its long edge) toward +Y (away from the robot base).
The hand positions before approach and after retreat are sampled in a 17 × 17 × 14 cm box around the reference start/return position: ±8.5 cm along each horizontal axis and ±7 cm vertically.
Planned hand-motion speeds: translation 0.10–0.12 m/s, slowing to approximately 0.01 m/s near grasp and placement; rotation up to 0.60 rad/s. Individual arm joints have speed limits of approximately 0.68–1.11 rad/s. Trajectories are filtered for arm reachability and collision constraints.

Robot-centric rendering

The head camera is moved by up to ±5 cm and rotated by ±5° along/about each axis relative to its calibrated pose. The wrist camera varies by ±2.5 mm and ±1° relative to its calibrated mounting pose.
Camera focal lengths, which control image magnification, are scaled by 0.90–1.10× (head) and 0.99–1.01× (wrist). The principal point—the image location of the camera’s optical axis—is shifted horizontally and vertically by ±20 px (head) or ±2 px (wrist).
Object colors: black, gray, white, ivory, green, red, blue, purple, and yellow, with one to three color regions. Textures include stripes, cross-stripes, speckles, marble, grain, and waves.
Lighting varies between warm, neutral, and cool tones, with different directions and shadow softness. Image brightness is scaled by approximately 0.81–1.23× for the head camera and 0.76–1.32× for the wrist camera. Red and blue image channels are scaled by 0.92–1.08× to vary color balance.
The rendered robot and tabletop brightness are separately scaled by 0.55–1.15× and 0.65–1.40× relative to their original appearance. Added shadows use a blending strength of 0–0.20.

Robot-centric rendering

The head camera is moved by up to ±5 cm and rotated by ±5° along/about each axis relative to its calibrated pose. The wrist camera varies by ±2.5 mm and ±1° relative to its calibrated mounting pose.
Camera focal lengths, which control image magnification, are scaled by 0.90–1.10× (head) and 0.99–1.01× (wrist). The principal point—the image location of the camera’s optical axis—is shifted horizontally and vertically by ±20 px (head) or ±2 px (wrist).
Object colors: black, gray, white, ivory, green, red, blue, purple, and yellow, with one to three color regions. Textures include stripes, cross-stripes, speckles, marble, grain, and waves.
Lighting varies between warm, neutral, and cool tones, with different directions and shadow softness. Image brightness is scaled by approximately 0.81–1.23× for the head camera and 0.76–1.32× for the wrist camera. Red and blue image channels are scaled by 0.92–1.08× to vary color balance.
The rendered robot and tabletop brightness are separately scaled by 0.55–1.15× and 0.65–1.40× relative to their original appearance. Added shadows use a blending strength of 0–0.20.

Whole-arm trajectory augmentation

Object positions are sampled 0.50–0.88 m from the calibrated robot base projected onto the tabletop plane, within a 65–130° sector intersected with the usable tabletop. Angles are measured from tabletop +X (along its long edge) toward +Y (away from the robot base).
The hand positions before approach and after retreat are sampled in a 17 × 17 × 14 cm box around the reference start/return position: ±8.5 cm along each horizontal axis and ±7 cm vertically.
Planned hand-motion speeds: translation 0.10–0.12 m/s, slowing to approximately 0.01 m/s near grasp and placement; rotation up to 0.60 rad/s. Individual arm joints have speed limits of approximately 0.68–1.11 rad/s. Trajectories are filtered for arm reachability and collision constraints.
